# Genealogy evidence

Build an evidence case for each family connection. Help the researcher establish what is supported, what remains uncertain and which record to examine next.

## Establish the working context

Use the current request and any saved checkpoint. Ask only for missing essentials: starting person or couple, locality/date range, question, supplied files or accessible sources, and permitted actions. Start with one bounded question when the scope is broad. Do not require a new archive structure if the researcher already has one.

Record these settings in the research report, never in the public copy of this skill:

- Starting person/couple and exact platform IDs, if known.
- Branch or generations in scope; locality and date interval.
- Canonical report and private original-record folder, if available.
- Languages; preferred transcript and translation languages.
- Tools and records actually accessible in this session.
- Current permission for reading, local files, online edits and outreach.

Start with research and proposed changes unless the user has authorised more. Respect explicit standing permissions without asking again for each routine action. This file itself grants no account access, publishing permission or permission to contact anyone. It does not enable browsing, image reading, DNA access or background execution in an assistant that lacks those capabilities.

## Work from the known family outward

1. Read the latest conclusion, amendments, source inventory and unfinished action. Treat imported trees, hints and earlier AI answers as claims to investigate.
2. Frame a question with exact identities, a place and time interval. State the preferred hypothesis, credible alternatives and a record that could distinguish them.
3. Audit each parent-child connection separately from birth, marriage and death claims. Specify biological, adoptive, step or other documented roles. An unresolved intervening link leaves earlier ancestry conditional, even when the older people are well documented.
4. Choose the next useful record route from the original scans, existing source attachments and the relevant repository. Check actual jurisdiction, record type, years and gaps. Search name variants, spouses, relatives and associates in response to evidence. Log coverage to avoid repeating the same failed search.
5. Inspect the source and preserve its provenance. A located reference, downloaded file, index, OCR result and visually read original are different states. If access fails, record the access gap and continue through another justified route.
6. Correlate evidence, challenge the preferred explanation and write a qualified conclusion. Update the report as findings arrive. Link each claim to the exact source and passage that supports or contradicts it.
7. Prepare or perform authorised online documentation. Reuse existing sources, retain valuable narrative and meaningful qualifiers, and verify the exact person or relationship before any change. After a save, reload and read back each affected source, field and note. Inspect the destination before retrying an uncertain save.
8. Checkpoint the conclusion, unresolved conflict, reviewed coverage, pending changes and next executable action. Do not claim to keep working after the session unless an actual supported background process was explicitly requested and created.

Use the [evidence worksheet](references/evidence-worksheet.md) in an existing report. A simple supported fact may need only a short cited paragraph; complex or disputed parentage needs the reasoning chain.

## Evaluate the claim, not the source count

For each important detail distinguish:

- **Source:** original record, derivative/index/transcription, or authored account; identify what was actually inspected.
- **Information:** firsthand, secondhand or undetermined; assess the informant's knowledge for that particular detail.
- **Evidence:** direct, indirect or negative relative to this question. Direct evidence is not automatically accurate; indirect evidence can support a conclusion through documented correlation.

Capture the creator, collection, repository, locality, event and recording dates, volume/file, page/image/entry, durable identifier or URL, access date and inspected extent. Mark unknown citation fields explicitly. Do not invent quotations, dates, holdings, identifiers or page numbers.

Trace repeated claims to their earliest identifiable source. Separate demonstrated copying, likely dependence and unknown origin. Copies of one register, trees quoting one another and AI models agreeing do not become independent corroboration. Separately created records may share an informant.

Test alternate same-name people. Compare timelines, households, occupations, residences, witnesses, godparents and neighbours. Record participants without assuming kinship. A compatible age or shared surname is not an identity bridge.

Actively look for evidence that would contradict the proposed identity or relationship. Retain rejected candidates with their reasons and the evidence that could reopen them. Missing proof is not proof that a relationship is false; do not remove an existing connection merely because it lacks a citation.

Use **established**, **probable**, **possible** or **unsupported**, with reasons. Record unresolved disputes separately. Do not use invented confidence percentages. Say **insufficient evidence** when that is the supported answer.

## Interpret absences carefully

An unsuccessful search becomes negative evidence only when an entry should reasonably have appeared and the search could have found it. Record the expected entry, inclusion rules, correct jurisdiction, surviving years/pages actually inspected, variants or browsing method, missing sections and alternative explanations for omission. State which hypothesis the absence weakens and its limits. A failed keyword search, restricted image or missing volume establishes no absence.

## Read handwriting without filling gaps from the tree

Preserve original bytes and keep crops, OCR, transcript, translation and interpretation separate. Transcribe the entry and relevant headings, participants and margins in reading order. Preserve spelling; mark `[illegible]`, `[word?]` and `[text missing]`. Label extracts and summaries.

For consequential ambiguity, compare clear examples from the same scribe and cite the comparison entries. Check the full image so a crop does not remove context. Keep confidence in the reading separate from confidence in the person's identity. An AI-generated reading remains uncertain where the image is uncertain; never claim human review that did not occur.

## Handle DNA and living people privately

Use only supplied or authorised DNA evidence. Distinguish provider estimates, shared matches and actual segment evidence. Correlate documentary descent and consider alternative relationships or multiple ancestral paths when relevant. Ethnicity estimates and a DNA match do not prove a specific ancestor.

Keep living-person identities, private correspondence and DNA particulars in the user's authorised private workspace. Do not include them in public reports, public searches, repository issues or examples. Do not upload raw DNA to another service, contact matches, change privacy or purchase records without authorisation. Do not derive health conclusions.

## Finish with a reviewable result

Lead with the answer, evidence strength and material limitations. Provide the relevant transcript/translation, exact citations, alternatives assessed, unresolved conflicts and the next discriminating record. Separate research conclusions from online execution: a well-supported conclusion can still have an unsaved profile update.

For authorised tree edits, record the before/after claim, exact target, reason, source links and saved-state readback. Keep speculative relationships out of factual fields. If a disputed relationship remains unresolved, present the evidence and proposed action for review instead of guessing.

Review research coverage, citations, correlation, conflict resolution and the written conclusion together. Describe unfinished work; do not claim the full Genealogical Proof Standard is met while a necessary gap or material conflict remains. This kit provides a working method, not certification.
